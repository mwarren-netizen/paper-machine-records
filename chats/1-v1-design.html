Drawing it surfaced some things I think are worth arguing about now:

Population and variation are two separate dials. I couldn't draw the run honestly with one encoding. The number of candidates drops, then rises again for the précis and the two referees. I'd sharpen the claim in one way. Each turn's freeze becomes the next turn's ground. Turns 2 and 4 bring in nothing new; they work from what the previous turn froze. Used as a checklist, the pattern finds four places where v0.1 departs from it:

  • Turn 3 has no variation step. Surviving lineages compete, but nothing generates rival contribution statements before compression picks one. That looks like a real gap.
  • Turns 1 and 4 fold reasons into judgment. No critic objects to a précis before the judges decide. This connects to your open question about how independent evaluators should be.
  • Turn 5 has a population of one. Selection becomes an acceptance test on edits, not a competition. I think that's a genuine change of regime rather than a gap, and the methodology report shouldn't claim the same operator all the way down.

Two consequences for the roles:

  • "Shape" disappeared. Once I regrouped the roles by your five operations, genre control stopped being a separate job. It's mostly the selection criteria tightening turn by turn.
  • Freeze has no worker. It's a controller decision recorded in state. I think that's correct, since it keeps freezing constitutional rather than philosophical.

The pushback: the loop by itself is generic. Popper's conjecture and refutation, genetic algorithms, and ordinary design iteration all draw this spiral. What distinguishes your machine is inside two of the five operations: reasons doing the selecting, and the ledger carrying those reasons forward. If the spiral becomes the headline image for the methodology prize, the caption has to carry that distinction.

The practical payoff for v0.2: the spec could become one operator, parameterized by turn. For each turn, the parameters are:

  • what varies;
  • what grounds it;
  • who gives reasons;
  • what the selection criteria are;
  • what freezes.

Then your open item on "exact prompts" becomes five prompt templates rather than fourteen stage prompts.

Where I'd push:

"No new philosophical moves" needs a test, not just an instruction. Emphasis and examples are exactly where a claim's scope drifts. A different example can quietly narrow a thesis, and a shift in emphasis can turn a qualified claim into an unqualified one. Your own framework gives the right test. Before variation, spec each unit by its inferential role: what it claims, what it relies on, and what it entitles later units to. A variant counts as mere exposition only if that role is unchanged. A local evaluator can check this per variant.

A frozen glossary of key terms helps too. Swapping a technical term for a near-synonym is the most common way prose variation changes content.

Unit-by-unit selection is local, but continuity is global. Picking each unit's best variant independently can yield a draft of good paragraphs that don't sit together. Terminology, cadence, and reused examples will clash. Select in sequence instead, judging each unit's variants against the already-chosen unit before it.

Note that smoothing and "light synthesis" are both unselected, single-candidate generation. That's where new moves sneak back in, so run the inferential-role check again after each smoothing pass.

Give each unit a word budget at the architecture freeze. Otherwise variants drift long. You lose proportion, and you find out about the 6,000-word cap at the audit instead of by design.

Set up the referee like a real one. Give it only the paper and the target's primary sources, never the target dossier or the ledger. Checking "representation of the target" against the machine's own reconstruction is circular.

I'd keep two reports from different model families, as v0.1 had.

Write the response letter as a real R&R response: accepted and revised, rebutted, or conceded as a limit. That letter becomes your liabilities ledger in genre form, and it's strong evidence for the methodology report. Anything conceded but unfixed should appear in the paper as an acknowledged limit. That's your guard against false completeness.

Define "genuinely fatal" before the report arrives. Otherwise you'll either reopen too readily or talk yourself out of it. A workable list:

  • the target is misrepresented at a proposition the thesis depends on;
  • a premise the contribution requires is unsupported and can't be supported;
  • the contribution already exists in the literature.

Everything else is local.

Put citation verification at the center of the audit. A fabricated reference or a misquoted source is the one error a judge will treat as disqualifying. Checking that each source exists and each quotation matches may need the human, under "supplying inaccessible literature." That's a constitutional check, not authorship, but it should be logged as such.

This is the right shape: reopening as a local widening of the radius, not a reset. I've rewritten the "Freezes are defeasible" note on the page to describe it that way. What's missing is a few pieces around the jump itself.

Diagnose the level before you widen. "Your reply depends on a distinction you never defend" can live at three different levels:

  • The defense may exist in the ledger but have been cut at compression or drafting. That's a turn 5 fix: restore it and re-budget the words.
  • The defense may exist but be weak. That's your turn 3 case.
  • The lineage may have no resources to defend the distinction at all. That's turn 2.

The ledger lets you tell these apart cheaply, so check it first. I suspect the first case will be common, because compression is exactly where load-bearing defenses get archived.

The risk runs the other way too: the machine will be tempted to diagnose the cheapest level. So give the diagnosis to a fresh evaluator, and have it state why the error lives there.

Put retreats in the repair pool, not just defenses. Some candidates should narrow the thesis instead, or remove the reply's dependence on the distinction, or concede the point as a stated limit. Often the strongest surviving repair is a smaller claim.

And you need a rule for when nothing survives. Then the contribution has lost at this level, and the reopening escalates again to turn 2.

Rerun the old objections, not just the new one. A repair that answers the referee can quietly reopen something answered back in dialectic. The ledger already holds every objection the contribution survived. Replaying them against the repaired version is the equivalent of software regression tests, and it's one of the strongest arguments for keeping the ledger at all.

The unit specs double as the dependency map. Last round I suggested speccing each argumentative unit by what it claims, relies on, and licenses. I think the file system is where this architecture either becomes executable or quietly degrades, so it's worth getting right before anything runs. I checked what the two apps actually allow, because four of their constraints shape the whole design.

What the two apps impose

You pick the model by hand, and you are the scheduler. Nothing I've found lets an instruction file switch models; you choose one each time you start a session. Cowork's scheduled tasks can't chain the steps for you either, because they can't be tied to a folder on your computer. That fits your decision not to build an orchestrator yet. What files can do is name the required model on each step card. The session's first act is then to check which model it's running on and stop if it's the wrong one.

The two apps read different instruction files. Once the folder is mounted, ChatGPT Work automatically reads an AGENTS.md file in it before performing any task. Cowork uses folder instructions that add project-specific context when you select a local folder, conventionally a CLAUDE.md file, which people swap for an AGENTS.md when moving the same folder structure to ChatGPT. So keep one canonical instruction set and make both files two-line pointers to it. Then the two models can never end up working from different constitutions.

Check which models Work offers in a local folder. One user reports that a local project can use Work chats with access to its local folder, but cannot use Chat with the ChatGPT/Pro model surface. Before assigning ChatGPT the judgment-heavy steps, open a local-folder Work session and see what the model menu actually gives you on Plus.

Memory features can quietly break independence. Memory is enabled for Cowork projects, so Claude can carry context from earlier tasks in a project into later ones. ChatGPT has its own memory too. Independent ruminations and fresh evaluators are the core of the architecture, and a memory that carries one session's ideas into the next defeats them without anyone noticing. Run the machine from a folder that isn't a memory-enabled project, or confirm you can turn memory off, on both sides.

The same logic rules out generating eight ruminations in one session: each would see the ones before it. Every independent generation needs its own session.

Three rules make the shared folder safe:

  • One app at a time. Never have both apps working in this folder at once.
  • Frozen files are never edited, only superseded. A reopening writes contribution_v2 beside v1, so every change of mind stays auditable.
  • Logs are append-only. In particular, human-interventions.md is what your methodology report will need to substantiate "minimal human collaboration."

How "run step N" works

PROTOCOL.md makes every session behave the same way:

  1. Check the order. Read STATE.md. If N isn't the next step, stop and ask, unless you've written an override.
  2. Check preconditions. Read the step card. Confirm the session is on the named model, the required inputs exist, and the last verdict wasn't red.
  3. Read only the step's whitelist. The card lists exactly which files the session may open. This is how "feed each role only the slice it needs" gets enforced, and how an evaluator stays blind to the process it's judging.
  4. Do the work. Write only the listed output files, and append to the ledger and run log.
  5. Report. Write a state-of-play report, update STATE.md, and end with a fixed completion block. The block gives the step, the verdict, the next step and its model, whether that step needs a fresh session, and any action needed from you.

Each step card has the same fields: purpose, turn and operation, required model, file whitelist, inputs, procedure, criteria file, outputs, exit test, and next step.

The state-of-play report covers:

  • what ran and on which model;
  • the population now, compared against the range in PARAMETERS.md;
  • what was allowed to vary, and whether anything strayed outside that;
  • which artifacts are frozen and at what version;
  • the count and top three of open liabilities;
  • deviations from design parameters;
  • the verdict.
