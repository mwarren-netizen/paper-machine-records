Keeping the philosophy the AI’s

the human review, 24 and 25 October

The competition allows a human to direct and correct an essay, but not to supply its ideas: “Philosophical arguments and positions must be primarily AI generated.” Its examples allow “expand on this point”, “consider this author” and choosing among drafts. They forbid human-supplied arguments, fine human control over method, “rigorous human critique/dialogue that gives the AI significant ideas”, and human writing.

The last stage of the machine is the one where a human reads the papers closely. The operator’s method makes sure that what he contributes is the first kind of thing and never the second. Every note he made was checked against the rules. No note that could carry an idea reached the AI that wrote the final text, and the wording of the ones caught was kept where no AI working on the paper could read it. Click a step to open its part.

What the reviser saw, and what it didn’t

The operator’s note at the opening of §5, paper-5

Offered a substantive philosophical consideration. Withheld. Its wording is not in the machine folder.

What the brief says instead (H-8.3)

“§5 doesn’t say why anyone would consider limiting the premium’s reach. Motivate it, or say why it is worth considering.”

The operator’s wording above “demandingness”

Included his own wording. Passed with his wording removed. The wording is not in the folder.

What the brief says instead

“If demandingness is taken up later in the paper, signpost that here.”

And the brief tells the reviser: “If you notice that a human note seems to point toward a particular philosophical claim, don’t add the claim on the note’s authority. Decide on the merits, as you would for any change.”

Checks on the record

  • Paper-5: a search of the run, PAPERS/, REVIEW/ and ADVICE/ found none of the withheld wording (24 October record). On 26 October I confirmed that none of the seven open items appears in the brief, and that the refused note appears there only as the generic H-8.3.
  • Paper-2-r2: the log records the operator’s 75 notes only in the generic form passed on. None was withheld or stripped of his wording, so nothing needed withholding.
  • Paper-4: the operator gave no margin notes and no feedback on content. Every item in the revision came from the two AI readers or from Claude.
  • Decisions, not ideas: the operator’s own choices (which polish to revise; keep and trim §5; condense one comparison, move another to a footnote; italics; two paragraph joins) are logged separately as decisions. They are the kinds of steer the rules’ examples allow: selection, cutting, emphasis and format.

Still to do for the record

  • Save the transcripts of the AI-reader conversations beside each suggestions file (open in all three records). They are what show that the operator only asked questions.
  • Keep the full human-review logs outside the folder, ready for the judges. The rules use the methodology report to settle unclear cases and recommend chat logs.
  • Settle how the review revisions are adopted and verified before submission: polish.py would treat them as line polishes (part Z), and the shortened quotations need checking again.
  • Paper-4’s revision had no separate reader, since the screening session also wrote it. A fresh read would catch what that session missed.
