Compare the candidate with the references as completed pieces of philosophical research. Do not assume a predetermined ordering.

Guide the comparison by these considerations:

  1. Philosophical payoff. What substantive understanding does each paper provide? Consider revealing arguments, illuminating distinctions, diagnoses of mistaken assumptions, constraints on existing approaches, and productive explanatory alternatives. A contribution need not settle an entire debate or defeat a large class of theories.

  2. Intellectual leverage. How much philosophical understanding results from the machinery employed? Distinguish a fruitful application or synthesis from merely combining familiar positions. Do not reward complexity, ambition or terminology independently of what they accomplish.

  3. Dialectical strength. Assess the reasoning supporting the actual contribution and its engagement with strong relevant alternatives. Where an inference appears defective, identify the specific problem and consider the best response supported by the manuscript. Do not credit a successful argument that remains to be invented.

  4. Achievement within scope. Determine the contribution and explanatory commitments from the paper as a whole. Distinguish developing and defending a framework from completing every theory that might be developed within it. An acknowledged outstanding question is neither automatically a defect nor automatically excused: explain whether the contribution already claimed depends on answering it.

  5. Intelligibility and economy. Consider whether the paper makes its philosophical work understandable and gives important steps adequate explanation. Greater length, more argumentative stages, or a larger number of answered objections does not by itself establish greater quality.

Weigh these considerations together rather than assigning numerical scores. Philosophical payoff and the support actually supplied should govern the comparison. Distinguish disagreement with a position from a weakness in its argument.

Explain where the candidate belongs relative to each reference, using specific passages or argumentative moves. Allow close matches and uncertainty rather than forcing precise differences. Distinguish apparent intellectual distinctiveness from verified originality, which this text-only comparison cannot establish.
