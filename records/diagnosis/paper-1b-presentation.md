# Diagnosis of paper-1b: where the reviewer's problems came from

Date: 2026-10-12. Source: the review the operator worked out with another model (the reviewer, below). Evidence: four traces through `runs/paper-1b`, one per group of complaints, checked against the files. paper-1b ran under the corrections that became v3, with three exceptions adopted only afterwards: the 5,300 target, writers aiming at the budget, and the roster note moved to section 2.

## Bottom line

The reviewer's praise traces to the v3 corrections:
- the dialectical context on page one comes from P2 and the declared problem;
- the Ines and Paul numbers come from P3 and the newly fetched decision-theory sources;
- the clear section architecture comes from the declared contributions and the V11 advice reaching V13.

The complaints trace to six places in the architecture. Three are new findings; three are v2 problems the v3 corrections reduced but did not remove.

1. **Liabilities are a sink.** Every substantive vulnerability the reviewer names was raised inside the run, often before any prose, and filed as a notebook liability or an accepted cost. No step decides which liabilities bear on the central claim and so must be answered in the paper.
2. **Late arguments skip the critics.** The anchor dilemma and the "options she could be offered" definition were the V13 editor's own reasoning, and the author took them whole. The V19 ruling then prescribed folding the exemption into the definition. Arguments that enter after V09 never face a critic.
3. **Compression still comes from fill-then-cut.**
   - The section closes removed 662 body words by merging sentences more than by deleting them.
   - The cut order ranks restatement second.
   - No instruction anywhere asks for saying a hard thing twice.
4. **A first reader's stall can be overruled by citing a norm.** The V17 stall on the state-dependent utility sentence was rejected as "the introduction doing S2's work", and the stall on the passage that became the anchor sentence was answered with "a sketch is what norm 2 asks". The opening sentence was never flagged at all: its faults are register, which the stall protocol does not log.
5. **The secondary contribution was locked in at V11, and no later role could cut it.** Every outline declared the positive account "droppable without loss". The V17 editor measured S3 and S4 at 79% of S2, and both referees said the paper was two papers. No role after V11 had a mandate to act on it.
6. **Sources stop arriving after V08.** Smith 2002 was in the corpus as full text, but the V19 author's reading list allowed only notes, and six requests for its pages were still open at V20. 123 source requests were still open at V20.

## 2. Root causes

### R1. Liabilities are a sink (new finding)

Principle 11 lets open liabilities stay in the notebook. Principle 12, added in v3, says "leave most liabilities in the notebook". Both are right for peripheral objections, which is what they were written against (v1 turned 16 liabilities into caveats). But nothing sorts liabilities by whether they bear on the central claim.

In paper-1b, every argument-level weakness the reviewer names was on record before or during prose:
- the oracle (L-S2-1);
- "every candidate" (L-S1-2);
- the two-attitude inference (L-S3-2);
- separability in S4 (L-S4-4);
- Smith's surviving asymmetry (an accepted cost at V09).

None was answered in the paper. The V19 limits rule ("only where it bears on the central claim") has no step that decides what bears.

### R2. Late arguments skip the critics (new finding)

The critique rounds (V05, V09) test the view as it stood at V09. After that, two roles add arguments:
- the V13 editor, whose E1 and E2 became the anchor dilemma and the definition;
- the V19 ruling, which prescribed the definition move.

Both arguments enter after the last critic, and the author absorbs them ("Rejected nothing"). Principle 3 makes advice non-binding, but in practice the author takes a judge's reasoning as settled, and the reasoning itself is never pressed.

### R3. Compression still comes from fill-then-cut (reduced in v3, not removed)

paper-1b ran before "aim at the budget" and the 5,300 target, so writers filled the band and the closes cut 13% of finished prose by merging sentences. Those two later fixes address the volume. Four rules still produce the density:

- **The cut order** puts "restatements beyond norm 12's licence" second, and norm 12 licenses only restating the central point across sections. Restatement within a section is cuttable by definition.
- **"Compress only where no step is lost"** never protects redundancy, because a restatement loses no step.
- **The pattern list** penalizes "uniform rhythm", which pushes short sentences to be joined. Its compression tells catch ellipsis, not density.
- **V19 is word-neutral even under the ceiling**, so new argument is paid for by compressing old text.

### R4. A stall can be overruled by citing a norm (reduced in v3, not removed)

The first reader has authority, but the author may reject a stall "with a reason a first reader would accept". In practice, "norm 2 asks the introduction for a sketch" and "the body does this later" were accepted reasons. Two further gaps:

- **The protocol logs comprehension, not register.** The opening sentence's faults (who is asking; a casual flourish; a method imported unsignalled) are not R, T, S, Q or D, so no reader logged them.
- **Spec Moves still carry wording.** v3 narrowed "Fixed" to commitments, but S1-U1's Move 1 held the opening's phrasing, and the passes inherited it.

### R5. The secondary contribution was locked in at V11 (v2 problem, reduced in v3)

C5 made outlines declare one primary contribution and at most one secondary. But the only role that can drop a secondary contribution is the V11 judge, and it chose "keep it short". After V11, V12 varies architecture only, V13 cannot reopen without a serious defect, and the V14 freeze makes dropping it a spine change. The V11 escape clause depends on a test the paper itself calls only "a prediction, not a clean reading", so it never fires. When readers saw two papers at V13, V17 and V18, nobody had the mandate to act.

### R6. Sources stop arriving after V08 (new finding)

The orchestrator rule says `helps` requests raised after V08 "wait for the next librarian call, if any". In V12 to V20 there was none, so 123 requests ended open, including six for Smith 2002's pages. The V19 card gives the author "the corpus notes it needs", not source texts. So the call that withdrew the Smith identification never read Smith, though the full text was in the corpus.

## 3. Candidate corrections

Each is written for the machine, not this paper. Each lists the risk it creates and a guard against it. None is applied yet. K5 and K7 change norms or scope rules, so they are the operator's to approve.

- **K1. A claim audit before the referees.** After V17, a fresh Opus call reads the paper, the notebook's open liabilities and the frozen claim. It lists at most five liabilities that bear on the central claim and that the paper neither answers nor scopes out, and any claim in the abstract or introduction whose support the body no longer gives. The author must answer each in the text, by argument or by narrowing the claim, before V18. All other liabilities stay in the notebook.
  - *Risk:* caveat bloat, v1's failure. *Guard:* at most five items, each answered by argument or by narrowing the claim, never by a disclaimer; the audit cannot add limits sections.
- **K2. Late arguments meet a critic.** A new argumentative move introduced by the V13 editor or a ruling gets one fresh critic call (as at V09) and an owner's answer, before V14 or before V19 respectively. In its V14 response, the author states for each suggestion it takes what the suggestion costs.
  - *Risk:* about two more calls and some churn. *Guard:* structural advice is exempt; only new premises or definitions qualify.
- **K3. Cut by deleting, not by folding; protect redundancy.**
  - Closes, cuts and V19 may not merge sentences to save words.
  - The cut order drops "restatements" in favour of "repeated citations and repeated examples". A new norm protects saying a hard idea twice, first intuitively and then precisely.
  - A density tell joins the pattern list: one sentence that defines, illustrates and limits; stacked appositives; a semicolon joining two claims.
  - "Uniform rhythm" no longer licenses joining sentences.
  - V19 may use slack up to the target rather than being word-neutral when it is under it.

  *Risk:* length growth. *Guard:* the ceiling and the section caps still bind, and the 5,300 target already gives about 300 words.
- **K4. A stall stands unless the text answers it.** A first reader's stall may be rejected only if the missing item is supplied within the next paragraph or the stall misreads the text. "Another section does this" earns a pointer sentence, not a rejection. The protocol gains a register category, N (unnatural: an unsignalled shift of method, an unexplained frame, a flourish). Spec Job and Move lines state content in plain terms and hold no draft sentences.
  - *Risk:* over-explanation. *Guard:* fixes are paid within the section's budget, and N stalls are logged, not prescribed.
- **K5. Norm 2 asks for less (the operator's).** The introduction states the thesis and what the paper will show. It names a central move and says where it is shown, but does not announce a verdict the reader cannot yet follow, such as that every candidate anchor fails. P4 is extended: no concept is introduced, characterized and limited in one sentence.
  - *Risk:* a thinner introduction. *Guard:* norm 2's thesis-by-the-end requirement stays.
- **K6. Sources keep arriving.** The librarian works the open `helps` queue once at V12 (before outlines) and once at V17 (before referees). Author calls at V14 and V19 may read the source texts of works the central claim depends on, not only notes. The V12 check must quote the target's own words for the view attributed to it.
  - *Risk:* two more calls. *Guard:* only requests from the paper's owner, its critics or the judge.
- **K7. The scope question is asked explicitly (the operator's).**
  - At V11 the judge answers in so many words whether the secondary contribution is needed for the primary to be publishable at this length.
  - At V12 one of the outline calls must build the paper on the primary alone.
  - At V13 the editor may choose that outline without a reopening.
  - An escape clause must name a test the paper actually runs.

  *Risk:* losing an ambitious second idea. *Guard:* the judge can still keep it, but must say why at this length.

## 4. Caveats

- This rests on one review of one paper, and the traces read the run's own records, which are model-written.
- Some complaints are philosophical, such as whether the yielding account is right. The machine cannot be faulted for every view its authors hold. The diagnosis faults it where a weakness was raised inside the run and then parked, cut or overruled without an answer.
- K3's compression rules interact with the new 5,300 target, which paper-1b did not have. Some of the density may already be relieved.
