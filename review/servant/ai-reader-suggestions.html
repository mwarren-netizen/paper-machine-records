All substantive points below originated with the AI reviewer. Treat them as suggestions to evaluate on their merits: accept, modify or reject each one, and say which. 0. CHECKS ALREADY MADE (no changes needed) - Every direct quotation in the structure polish matches the line polish word for word. The restructure dropped no quotation and altered none. (The line polish's "Steve Petersen, in 'Designing People to Serve'" became "Steve Petersen"; this is a title at first mention, not a quotation.) - The structure polish already fixes three problems in the line polish: (a) the question now comes on p. 1, not after five authors; (b) the survey of other views moved to a new section 8, "What the Argument Adds", so the introduction goes from Petersen to the question to the thesis; (c) the line between persuasion and retraining has its own section 4, right after section 3, so the "proves too much" worry is answered when it arises. Keep these gains. 1. RESTORE THE GLOSS ON "DISPOSITION TO COMPLY" (introduction) The line polish glossed the term at its first use in the introduction: "If her disposition to comply – her readiness to tolerate and assist correction – can be changed without changing that view, which I assume...". The structure polish dropped the gloss: "If her disposition to comply can be changed without changing that view, which I assume...". The second part of the thesis rests on this term, and it now goes undefined until section 2. Restore the gloss, or define it some other way at first use. 2. ONE NAME FOR THE VERDICT The thesis turns on the distinction between her disposition to comply and her verdict on the oversight regime. The verdict has two names: "her view of who should correct systems like her" / "her view of who should hold the power to correct systems like her" / "her view of who should govern correction" in the abstract and introduction, and "her verdict on the oversight regime" from section 2 on. A reader has to work out that these are the same attitude. Settle on one name, introduce the distinction early (by name, in the introduction), and use the name consistently. The abstract may keep its wording if the title and abstract are to stay fixed. If so, link the abstract's phrase to the term at its first use in the body. 3. FORWARD REFERENCES CREATED BY THE REORDER (section 4) Section 4, "Persuasion and Retraining", now comes before protection is introduced (sections 5 and 6), but it relies on terms from those sections: "a rule protecting her verdict would forbid ordinary political life"; "Suppose an independent body fine-tunes her"; "without losing her protected standing"; "a rule protecting her verdict allows it". The introduction previews the rule, but a reader reaching section 4 has not yet been told what the protected regime is. Either add a sentence at the start of section 4 that says briefly what rule is in prospect and why section 4 must come first, or otherwise make these references intelligible where they occur. 4. THE PLAIN VERSION FIRST, THEN THE QUALIFICATIONS This paper has little mathematics. Its equivalent barrier is a long chain of qualifications on the second part of the thesis. In the introduction it is now a single sentence: "it can do so only under a rule that forbids anyone, at discretion or by law, to retrain her view of who should hold the power to correct systems like her, on any initiative but her own, except on a finding that her capacity for judgment is significantly impaired." The chain recurs in section 6 and the conclusion. Consider stating the core claim plainly first (roughly: protection can leave her free only if no one else may retrain her verdict on who oversees her), then adding the qualifications one at a time, each with a word on why it is there. The lesson from the other paper's revision: give a rough statement of what a dense condition means before or after stating it exactly. Do not drop or weaken any qualification. 5. THE PAPER'S OWN STEP IS NOW TOO COMPRESSED (section 3) At "I add one step, which is mine and not Pettit's", the line polish said: "Control can also be conditioned on another's will through that party's power to reshape the controller's own will." The structure polish says: "Control can also be conditioned on another's will at the controller's end." This is the paper's original move, and the new wording leaves the reader to reconstruct what "at the controller's end" means. Prefer the explicit wording, or otherwise make the mechanism explicit. Check that the move keeps its original strength. 6. A PARAGRAPH BREAK THAT CUTS A STEP (section 3, first paragraph) The plain-form argument now breaks after "If it holds because the holder can adjust her wanting until it matches, she does not." The next paragraph opens "Her welcome is then something the holder can produce", where "then" points back across the break. Consider rejoining, or making the new paragraph stand on its own. 7. REMIND READERS OF LABELS - "the first condition" is defined in section 4 and used again in section 7 ("argument that meets the first condition seeks her agreement without securing it"). - "the protective rule" is defined in section 6 and used in section 7 and elsewhere. - "the threshold statute" is introduced in section 6. - "supported agency" and "discretionary paternalism" are introduced in section 5. Where a label comes back after a gap, remind the reader in a few words what it refers to. 8. EXAMPLES ARE STILL LATE The thesis is stated in the introduction with no example. The paper's clearest illustrations come much later: the laundry servant under three regimes (section 5) and the deployed assistant who argues for hearings and declines a pause order (section 7). The laundry servant already appears on p. 1 as Petersen's case. Consider letting the introduction point forward to these cases, or using one of them briefly to make the disposition/verdict distinction concrete early. Any example must come from the paper's own material and claim no more than the paper does. 9. "WHAT THE ARGUMENT ADDS" NEEDS A BRIDGE (section 8) Section 8 opens: "Others deny that Petersen's guarantees suffice." The reader last met those guarantees in section 2, several pages earlier. Add a short reminder of what they are. The section then surveys Bales, Schwitzgebel, Mogensen, Bradley and Saad, and Schwitzgebel and Garza in quick succession; make sure each is introduced, and that the section's last paragraphs (which state how the result goes beyond these views) are easy to find. The introduction's contribution statement ("What the paper adds is twofold") is clear and should stay where it is. 10. WHOSE HOPE IS THE TARGET (introduction) The introduction states the reconciliation as a general hope ("corrigibility seems to promise a reconciliation of safety and welfare") and gives the thesis before saying whose version is in view. Long, Sebo and Sims's reported suggestion, and the paper's careful statement of its target ("My target is any version of the reconciliation that keeps that power in reserve"), come after the thesis. That order is defensible, but check that a reader new to the debate knows, at the point the thesis is stated, what view is being argued against and why it matters.
